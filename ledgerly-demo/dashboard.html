Ang Mo Kio Logistics is a last-mile delivery-as-a-service business: it bills retail and F&B clients (Bishan Home Services, Tampines Retail Group, and others in the Receivables tab) for delivery fulfillment, and pays gig drivers on Grab/foodpanda/Lalamove as its direct cost of delivering it. "Retail revenue" and "F&B revenue" throughout this demo are service revenue from those client segments -- not product sales -- and gig payroll is the cost of fulfilling them, the same way Grab or foodpanda takes a cut of what a merchant is billed and pays a smaller amount to the driver.

8-Week Closing Cash Trend
Every number on this screen traces to a specific tab in this demo — the forecast, the payroll roster, receivables, or the scenario planner. Nothing here is invented independently.
How the tabs connect:
  • Gig Payroll Roster and Receivables are the real inputs — actual amounts and dates. Uncheck a driver on the Roster tab (out sick, etc.) and their real payout and revenue share come out of every projection below, live.
  • 13-Week Forecast aggregates those into weekly revenue/cost lines.
  • Scenario Planner takes those same lines and perturbs them by driver assumptions (churn, new clients, fuel, late payment) to project 8 weeks forward.
  • This Overview page rolls up numbers from all of the above.
  • AP Aging is a point-in-time snapshot of what's currently owed. Adding a vendor there is a real payable, not a hypothetical — it lands in the snapshot immediately and also feeds every projection, unlike the Worst/Base/Best scenario selector which only toggles between assumptions.

Scenario Planner

As of 2026-09-07 — driver-based modeling, projecting 8 weeks forward from today. Change the scenario, the whole projection recalculates live.
How this projection is actually calculated
  1. Starts from the same real weekly revenue and cost lines used in the 13-Week Forecast tab (retail, F&B, gig payroll, fuel, fixed costs) -- not a separate number.
  2. Driver churn reduces both revenue and gig payroll cost, using the actual average revenue and payout per driver computed from the Gig Payroll Roster tab -- losing a driver costs more in lost revenue than it saves in payroll, which is why churn is net-negative.
  3. New client win adds straight to weekly revenue.
  4. Fuel volatility scales the real fuel/vehicle-maintenance line from the forecast by the selected percentage.
  5. Late-paying client delay drags against the real total open receivables from the Receivables tab, spread evenly across the 8-week window.
  6. Each week's balance = last week's balance + (adjusted revenue − adjusted costs). Change the dropdown above and every number here, including the chart, recalculates from these same rules.
  7. Driver exclusions (Gig Payroll Roster tab) and added vendors (AP Aging tab) feed into this same calculation too — they're real, not hypothetical, so they apply on top of whichever scenario is selected, not instead of it.
